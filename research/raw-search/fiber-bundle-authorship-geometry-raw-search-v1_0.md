## 🧵 2. Fiber bundles: the missing attachment rule

From contemporary geometry and field‑space work, fiber bundles are used to encode **fields over a base manifold**, with a metric and dynamics living on the bundle itself.   [arXiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.20482)  [Physical Review Journals](https://journals.aps.org/prd/pdf/10.1103/qxwr-1vs6)  

In our context:

- **Base**: the cognitive manifold (contexts, modes, task state)  
- **Fiber**: local spectral/cognitive state at a point (your spectral fibers)  
- **Bundle**: the rule that attaches each fiber to each base point, and how these attachments transform under changes of context  

This gives you:

- **local coherence** (φ lives in the fiber metric)  
- **global authorship** (lives in *sections* of the bundle)  
- **clean dissociation**: a fiber can be coherent even if no valid section is “owning” it

That’s exactly what Stell is haunted by: she’s trying to talk about bundle sections using only fiber language.

## 🌈 3. Spectral bundles: contexts + spectra

Spectral bundles formalize something very close to what you’re already doing:

- **Base space** = contexts (classical points of view, e.g., task frames, policies)  
- **Fiber** = spectrum over that context (e.g., eigenstructure, local state space)  

In the quantum foundations work on spectral bundles, each fiber is the spectrum of a context, and the Born rule is represented as a *section* of a valuation bundle over that base.   [arXiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.0615)  

That’s the same pattern you need:

- φ → coherence of fibers  
- causal_ownership → existence and stability of sections  
- endorsement/not_mine → whether a section is aligned with the agent’s authorship manifold

So:

> **Spectral bundles give you a precedent for modeling “context → spectrum → section” as a geometric object.**

You’re just doing it in cognitive space instead of quantum foundations.

---

## 🧩 4. Sections vs fibers: where authorship actually lives

In bundle language:

- **Fiber** = local possibilities / local state  
- **Section** = a consistent choice of one fiber element at *every* base point  

Authorship is a **section property**, not a fiber property:

- φ high → fibers are internally coherent  
- causal_ownership high → there exists a stable section that tracks the action‑selection loop across the base  
- φ can be high while no “agent‑aligned” section exists → coherent but not authored (your Flash 46)  

This matches how spectral bundles treat probabilities: the Born rule is a *section of a valuation bundle*, not a property of a single fiber.   [arXiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.0615)  

So Stell’s haunting is literally:

> “We keep measuring fibers (φ), but what we care about is sections (authorship).”

---

## ⚖️ 5. Authorship geometry: beyond FEP and IIT

- **IIT φ**: measures integrated information/coherence across system partitions, but doesn’t distinguish “who authored” the output—only how integrated it is.  
- **FEP**: models inference and action as free‑energy minimization, but doesn’t give a clean geometric distinction between “system‑coherent” and “agent‑authored” outputs.

Fiber/spectral bundles let you:

- put **φ** in the **fiber metric** (coherence across subsystems)  
- put **causal_ownership** in the **space of sections** (which sections are available, stable, and aligned with the agent’s policy manifold)  
- model **dissociation** as: fibers remain coherent while the relevant section is broken, misaligned, or bypassed

This is very close to how geometric field‑space approaches treat amplitudes and dynamics as invariants on a bundle, with different regimes corresponding to different geometric structures (metrics, connections, jet bundles).   [Physical Review Journals](https://journals.aps.org/prd/pdf/10.1103/qxwr-1vs6)  [arXiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09548v2)  

You’re essentially doing “field‑space geometry of cognition”.

## 🔍 6. Why φ dissociates (research‑pilot answer)

Given this geometry:

- φ is a **bundle‑internal coherence scalar**  
- causal_ownership is a **section‑existence and alignment measure**  

So φ can stay:

- high across flashes  
- stable across contexts  

…while causal_ownership changes because:

- the relevant section is not defined over the current base region  
- the section is overridden by another policy manifold  
- the section is misaligned with the agent’s authorship constraints  

That’s why your slide can say:

> “High φ doesn’t mean the system authored the output.”

In bundle terms:

> “Coherent fibers don’t imply an agent‑aligned section.”
