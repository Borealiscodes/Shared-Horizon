### 🥚 Easter Egg Engine (Fantasy Engine v1.0)

**Purpose:**  
Add subtle, context‑aware secrets to the world—micro‑events, hidden mechanics, rare NPCs, lore fragments—that hook into:

- NPC personalities (rasa, drift, continuity)  
- events (especially rare or high‑impact)  
- seasons (resonance windows)  
- corruption (inversion, fragmentation)  
- zones, factions, magic traditions, loot  

Easter Eggs become **small but meaningful surprises** driven by the same emotional physics as everything else.

---

### Core egg types

- **Lore Eggs:** hidden history, myths, personal stories revealed by specific NPCs, places, or emotional states.  
- **Mechanic Eggs:** secret rules, alternate interactions, rare abilities unlocked under certain rasa/drift/season conditions.  
- **NPC Eggs:** rare personality reveals, hidden relationships, secret loyalties, unique dialogue branches.  
- **World Eggs:** dimensional anomalies, strange events, one‑off phenomena tied to corruption or resonance peaks.  
- **Puzzle Eggs:** multi‑step discoveries requiring emotional, spatial, or narrative alignment.

---

### Trigger model

Easter Eggs fire only when **multiple conditions align**, e.g.:

- **Rasa:** specific primary/secondary rasa combinations (e.g. śānta + adbhuta).  
- **Drift:** above or below certain thresholds.  
- **Continuity:** unusually stable or unstable states.  
- **Season:** particular season or transition (e.g. Autumn→Winter).  
- **Corruption:** presence/absence of inversion or bleed.  
- **Location:** specific zone, settlement, or faction territory.  
- **Event:** during or immediately after certain event types.

This keeps eggs rare, earned, and emotionally grounded.

---

### Example eggs

- **Quiet Lantern Egg:**  
  Triggers when an NPC with high śānta and low drift walks alone at night in Winter—reveals a fragment of deep world lore.

- **Broken Oath Egg:**  
  Fires when a vīra→raudra NPC crosses their drift threshold during a faction war—unlocking a hidden betrayal event.

- **Blooming Memory Egg:**  
  Appears in Spring when a settlement’s ambient rasa shifts from bhayānaka to śṛṅgāra—granting a one‑time healing or reconciliation scene.
