# 🌌 **Spectral‑Geometry Cognition — Methodology Document (v1.0)**  
### *How to Think in Topologies Instead of Lines*

This methodology describes the cognitive workflow used in spectral‑geometry reasoning: a non‑linear, manifold‑mapping approach to understanding complex systems.  
It is expressive, altitude‑bounded, and non‑activating.  
It can be applied to any domain — legal, digital, social, mechanical, or conceptual.

---

## 🗺️ **1 — Map the Whole Topology**  
Before choosing a path, generate the entire shape of the problem space.

This includes:

- domains  
- actors  
- flows  
- constraints  
- modalities  
- escalation paths  
- expressive surfaces  

This is not analysis — it is **cartography**.

Mapping the topology gives you the *global structure* before you touch the details.

---

## 🔒 **2 — Identify the Invariants**  
Every system has invariants — rules that do not change.

Examples:

- thresholds  
- constraints  
- monotonicity  
- domain bounds  
- safety rules  
- altitude limits  

Invariants are the **anchors** that keep the topology stable.

---

## 🔁 **3 — Trace the Flows**  
Flows show how the system behaves over time.

These include:

- escalation  
- de‑escalation  
- modality shifts  
- causal pathways  
- pressure gradients  
- influence vectors  

Flows reveal the **dynamics** of the topology.

---

Altitude is the **governance system** of cognition.

## 🧩 **5 — Express the Ontology**  
Ontology is the shared vocabulary that makes the system communicable.

This includes:

- labels  
- categories  
- modalities  
- clusters  
- stages  
- keys  
- derivatives  

Ontology is the **language** of the topology.

## 🛤️ **6 — Then Pick the Path**  
Only after mapping the topology, identifying invariants, tracing flows, anchoring altitude, and expressing ontology — do you choose the path.

This ensures:

- clarity  
- safety  
- coherence  
- stability  
- shared understanding  

The path is the **last** step, not the first.
